# Research Ethics and Consent Plan

## Project Title

**Evaluating Collaboration Tools for Effective Virtual IT Project Management: A Comparative Study of Jira, Microsoft Teams and Slack**

---

# 1. Purpose

This document describes the ethical considerations and participant-consent procedures for the research project.

The project may collect primary research data from people with experience of virtual or hybrid IT project environments.

Primary data collection will only begin after the researcher has confirmed and complied with the applicable university research-ethics requirements.

---

# 2. Ethical Principles

The research will be conducted according to the following principles:

* Voluntary participation
* Informed consent
* Respect for participants
* Confidentiality
* Data minimisation
* Secure data handling
* Transparency
* Right to withdraw where applicable
* Avoidance of harm
* Honest reporting of findings

---

# 3. Ethical Approval

Before collecting primary research data, the researcher will confirm the requirements of the relevant university or academic institution.

Where formal ethics approval is required, data collection will not begin until the required approval has been obtained.

The researcher will follow any conditions attached to the approval.

# 4. Participant Eligibility

Potential participants should ideally:

* Have experience working in IT or technology-related environments.
* Have experience working in virtual or hybrid teams.
* Have used at least one collaboration or project-management tool.

Potential participants may include:

* IT project managers
* Project coordinators
* Scrum Masters
* Software developers
* Software engineers
* Business analysts
* Product owners
* IT consultants
* Other IT professionals

The final eligibility requirements will be confirmed in accordance with university requirements.

---

# 5. Recruitment

Participants may be recruited through appropriate channels such as:

* University networks
* Professional networks
* LinkedIn
* Personal professional contacts
* Relevant IT communities
* Academic contacts

Recruitment messages will clearly explain:

* The purpose of the study
* Who is eligible
* What participation involves
* Approximate completion time
* Voluntary nature of participation
* How the data will be used

Participants will not be pressured to participate.

---

# 6. Participant Information

Before participating, individuals should receive appropriate information about the research.

The information should explain:

### Research Purpose

The study investigates how collaboration tools support virtual IT project management.

### Participation

Participants may be asked to complete an online questionnaire and, where applicable, participate in a short interview.

### Expected Time

The questionnaire is expected to take approximately **8–12 minutes**.

Interview duration will be communicated separately before an interview takes place.

### Voluntary Participation

Participation is voluntary.

Participants may choose not to participate.

### Research Use

Responses will be used for academic research and project analysis.

---

# 7. Informed Consent

Participants should provide informed consent before completing the research activity.

Consent should confirm that the participant:

* Has read the participant information.
* Understands the purpose of the research.
* Understands what participation involves.
* Understands that participation is voluntary.
* Understands how their data will be handled.
* Agrees to participate.

The exact consent wording will be reviewed against the requirements of the relevant university.

---

The final consent wording will be confirmed with the relevant university requirements.

---

# 9. Confidentiality

The research will aim to protect participant confidentiality.

The project will avoid publishing information that could unnecessarily identify individual participants.

Where appropriate, results will be reported in aggregated form.

For example:

> "The majority of participants rated communication effectiveness highly."

rather than identifying an individual participant.

---

# 10. Personal Data

The project will apply data minimisation principles.

The researcher will avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.

The questionnaire should not request:

* Passwords
* Personal addresses
* Confidential company information
* Private project information
* Unnecessary identifying information

If demographic information is collected, it should only be collected where it is relevant to the research objectives.

---

# 11. Company and Organisational Confidentiality

Participants should not be asked to disclose confidential information belonging to their employer or organisation.

The questionnaire should focus on general experiences rather than confidential project details.

Participants will be instructed not to include:

* Client names
* Employer-confidential information
* Internal project documentation
* Security credentials
* Commercially sensitive information
* Personal information about colleagues

---

# 12. Data Storage

Research data will be stored securely.

The researcher will maintain appropriate controls over access to raw participant data.

Potential storage locations may include approved university storage or secure local storage, depending on university requirements.

The researcher will follow the institution's requirements concerning:

* Storage location
* Access control
* Retention period
* Data deletion
* Backup procedures

---

# 13. GitHub and Research Data

GitHub will be used for project version control and documentation.

However:

**Raw participant data will not be uploaded to a public GitHub repository.**

This includes:

* Raw questionnaire responses
* Interview recordings
* Interview transcripts containing identifiable information
* Email addresses
* Names
* Consent records
* Other personal information

The public repository may contain:

* Analysis code
* Documentation
* Anonymised datasets where permitted
* Synthetic datasets
* Charts
* Aggregated results
* Project-management documents

Any dataset shared publicly will first be checked to ensure that it does not reveal participant identities or confidential information.

---

# 14. Data Anonymisation

Where appropriate, participant data will be anonymised or pseudonymised before analysis or reporting.

For example:

Instead of:

**John Smith — Software Developer — Company X**

the analysis may use:

**Participant P001 — Software Developer**

The final approach will depend on the university's data-protection requirements.

---

# 15. Interview Data

If interviews are conducted, participants will be informed about:

* Whether the interview will be recorded.
* How recordings will be stored.
* Who will have access.
* Whether interviews will be transcribed.
* How long the data will be retained.
* How recordings will eventually be deleted.

Interview recording will only take place where appropriate consent has been obtained.

---

# 16. Right to Withdraw

Participants will be informed about whether and how they can withdraw from the research.

The withdrawal procedure and deadline will be clearly communicated to participants before data collection.

The final withdrawal wording will follow university ethics requirements.

---

# 17. Potential Risks to Participants

The project is expected to involve minimal risk.

Potential risks may include:

* Discomfort when discussing workplace experiences.
* Accidental disclosure of confidential information.
* Concern about how responses may be interpreted.

To reduce these risks:

* Participants will not be required to provide confidential company information.
* Questions will focus on general experiences.
* Participants may skip questions where permitted.
* Responses will be handled confidentially where applicable.

---

# 18. Researcher Responsibilities

The researcher is responsible for:

* Obtaining required ethical approval.
* Providing participant information.
* Obtaining appropriate consent.
* Protecting participant data.
* Following university research policies.
* Storing data securely.
* Reporting findings honestly.
* Avoiding fabricated data.
* Avoiding manipulation of results.
* Maintaining appropriate research records.

---

# 19. Data Analysis Integrity

The researcher will not:

* Fabricate participant responses.
* Delete results simply because they do not support the expected conclusion.
* Manipulate scores to make one tool appear better.
* Misrepresent participant opinions.
* Present hypothetical data as real participant data.

Unexpected or contradictory findings will be reported where relevant.

---

# 20. Publication and Presentation

Research findings may be included in:

* Academic reports
* Project presentations
* GitHub project documentation
* Academic assessment materials

Only information that is appropriate for publication will be included.

Participant identities and confidential information will not be disclosed without appropriate justification and permission.
